# MiZanova — User Manual

**For families, teachers, specialists, school administrators and Special Miles staff.**

MiZanova is a support platform for neurodiverse students in Australian schools.
It keeps one shared picture of a child — their goals, the observations made at
school and at home, their plan, and the conversations between the adults
supporting them — and shows each person exactly the part of it that is theirs
to see.

This manual is organised by **who you are**. Read *Before you start*, which
applies to everybody, then the one chapter for your role. You do not need any
of the others.

| If you are… | Read |
|---|---|
| A parent or guardian | [Chapter 1 — Parents and guardians](#chapter-1--parents-and-guardians) |
| A teacher | [Chapter 2 — Educators](#chapter-2--educators) |

# Before you start

## What you can see, and why

The most important thing to understand about MiZanova is that **it does not
show everyone the same screen with different things hidden**. What reaches your
device is decided in the database, before it is sent. A teacher does not
receive a record about a child who is not theirs; a parent does not receive a
row about anybody else's child.

This matters in one practical way: if a screen looks empty, that is a fact
about your access or about the data, not a display setting you can change.
Where something is genuinely missing rather than simply not yours, the screen
says so in those words.

## The six kinds of account

| Role | Who it is for | Chosen by |
|---|---|---|
| **Parent** | A parent or guardian of a child at the school | Redeeming a code from the school |
| **Educator** | A classroom teacher | Invited by their school |
| **Specialist** | A learning or wellbeing specialist | Invited by their school |
| **School Admin** | Whoever runs the school's use of MiZanova | Set up by Special Miles |
| **Platform Admin** | Special Miles staff | Set up by Special Miles |
| **Student** | A young person, seeing their own goals | Granted by their parent |

**You never choose your own role.** It comes from your account, and the sign-in
page deliberately does not ask. If you think your role is wrong, it is your
school — not MiZanova support — who changes it.

## How you get an account

You cannot create one yourself. An account is created *by the thing that gives
you a reason to have one*, so that everybody who has one is already connected
to a school or a child from the moment they arrive. There are three doors:

**1. Your child's school gave you a code.**
It looks like `K7QP-4M2X-9RTB`. Go to **mizanova.app/link**, enter it, and it
sets up your account and links your child in one step. The code works **once**,
expires after **30 days**, and only works for the email address the school
issued it to.

**2. Your school invited you to work there.**
Teachers, specialists and school staff are invited by email. Open the link in
that email — it already knows who you are and which school you belong to, so
there is nothing to fill in and no role to pick. Do not sign up separately
first; an account made that way cannot be attached to the invitation.

**3. You want MiZanova for your school.**
Special Miles sets the school up and creates the first administrator account.
From there the school invites its own staff and families. Start at the
**Pricing** page and get in touch.

> **If you have none of these**, ask your child's school office for a code, or
> ask them to invite you if you work there. Only a school can do either, and
> that is what keeps a child's record reachable by the right people and nobody
> else.

> explaining how to get an account.

## Signing in

Go to **mizanova.app/login** and enter your email address and password.

- **Show password** reveals what you have typed, if you are not sure.
- **Forgot password?** emails you a reset link.
- There is no "sign in with Google or Microsoft", and there is no role to
  choose.

If the details are wrong you will see *"That email and password do not match an
account."* — deliberately the same message whether the address is unknown or
the password is wrong.

**Signing in is the one thing that needs an internet connection.** Only the
server can check a password. If you are offline you will be told so plainly;
everything else works once you are back in.

> **[Screenshot: the login page]**

## Two-factor authentication

Two-factor authentication asks for a six-digit code from an app on your phone,
as well as your password.

**It is required** for educators, specialists, school administrators and
platform administrators — everyone who can open records about identifiable
children. A stolen password should not be enough on its own.

**It is optional for parents**, who see their own child and nobody else's, and
who should not be locked out of the daily summary because they changed phones.
You can still turn it on, and it is worth doing.

When you set it up you are shown **recovery codes**. Save them somewhere that
is not your phone — they are how you get back in if you lose the device. SMS
codes are not offered.

Set this up at **Settings → Security**.

> **[Screenshot: Settings → Security, two-factor section]**

## Your account settings

Reach these from your own name in the app. Three tabs, the same for every role:

**Profile**
- *Your details* — your name and photo, as colleagues and families see you.
- *Email address* — what you sign in with, and where invitations and password
  resets go.
- *Role and session* — set by the platform, not by you. Shown so you know where
  you stand.
- *Other devices* — **signs out every other session and leaves this one alone.**
  Worth doing if you have used a shared or classroom computer.
- *Notifications on this device* — see below.

**Security** — two-factor authentication, recovery codes, and changing your
password. Your current password is checked before a change is made.

**School** — the details your school is the authority on. Read-only: changing
them is a conversation with your school, not a form here. If your account is
not attached to a school yet, this tab says so.

> **[Screenshot: Settings → Profile]**

## Notifications on a device

MiZanova can send a notification to a phone or computer when something needs
you.

**A notification tells you how many things need you and which school. It never
names a child or says what happened.** These screens are open on shared
classroom machines, and a notification can be read on a locked screen by
whoever is walking past.

Turn it on at **Settings → Profile → Notifications on this device**.

**Each browser is separate.** Turning it on at school does not turn it on at
home. If the button is missing or does nothing, the screen tells you which of
three reasons applies: the browser cannot receive notifications at all (on an
iPhone, that is every browser unless you have added the site to your home
screen), the deployment has no notification keys set up yet, or you have
blocked notifications for this site in the browser itself — which has to be
undone in the browser's own settings, usually behind the padlock in the address
bar.

## Using MiZanova offline

The app opens and works without a connection: screens you have already visited
are stored on the device. Two things genuinely cannot work offline — **signing
in**, and anything that has to reach the server for fresh data. Where that
happens the screen says the information could not be loaded, rather than
showing you an empty list as though there were nothing there.

## Getting help

The **Help** page in the footer, and the **Status** page, are open to everyone
without signing in. For anything about your child, your class or your account,
your school is the right first stop.

# Chapter 1 — Parents and guardians

Your account shows you your own child or children and nothing else. If more
than one child is linked to you, a picker at the top of each screen chooses
between them.

Everything a parent can reach is in the left-hand menu, grouped as **Your
child**, **Keeping in touch** and **Your account**.

## Home

Where you land after signing in. Written to be read on a phone, standing up.

It brings together, in one screen:

- **Recent updates from school** — behaviour observations a teacher has chosen
  to share with you. Teachers decide what to share; if nothing is here yet, the
  screen says so rather than implying there has been nothing to report.
- **Coming up** — the next appointments for your child.
- **Goal progress** — how the current goals are tracking.
- **Your home observations** — what you have written, and a button to add one.

Your child is shown by first name throughout. That is deliberate: a screenshot
of this page shared in a group chat carries no surname.

> **[Screenshot: Parent Home]** — the whole dashboard on a phone-width screen.

## Progress Highlights

Two things:

- **Skill progress** — your child's goals grouped by category, with the
  percentage worked out from milestones the school has ticked off.
- **Recent highlights** — milestones actually completed, together with what you
  yourself have written.

**Print or save as PDF** produces a progress report. The date on it is the date
it was *printed*, said plainly on the page, because a report that looks like an
official document should not be vague about when it was made.

There is deliberately **no behaviour trend chart** here. You only see behaviour
logs a teacher chose to share, so a trend drawn from them would be computed
from a partial sample — "incidents are down this month" might only mean fewer
were shared. A chart that looks like data but is not would be worse than none,
especially one you might act on.

> **[Screenshot: Progress Highlights]**

## Goals & IEP

Your child's goals and their Individual Education Plan, **exactly as the school
staff see them**. There is no separate parent-friendly summary that could drift
away from the real document.

This screen is read-only. You contribute through home observations and
messages; the plan itself is written by the school.

Where a document asks you to confirm you have read it, doing so **records the
date and time**. That record is the point of it, so it is worth reading before
you press.

> **[Screenshot: Goals & IEP]**

## Home Observations

**The one screen where you write rather than read.** Something that worked at
home, something that did not, a pattern you have noticed — the staff supporting
your child see it immediately.

To add one:

1. Open **Home Observations** and press **Log an observation**.
2. **What happened?** — a short title, e.g. *"Improved morning routine
   independence"*.
3. **Tell us more** — what you saw, and anything that seemed to help.
4. **Category** — one of *Social & emotional*, *Language*, *Motor*, *Sensory*,
   *Cognitive*, or *Other*.
5. **When did it happen?** — defaults to today; it cannot be in the future.
6. Press **Share with school**.

Unlike a teacher's behaviour log — which is hidden from you until they choose
to share it — **an observation you write is visible to your child's assigned
staff straight away**. You wrote it precisely so the school would see it.

You can correct one afterwards: open it, edit, and press **Save the
correction**. Past observations are listed underneath with a search box.

> **[Screenshot: the "Log an observation" form]**

## Appointments

When your child is being seen, and by whom.

This screen is **read-only**. Knowing when your child is seen is ordinary;
moving a clinician's calendar from a parent's phone is a different thing. To
change an appointment, tell the person who booked it.

Two things every card tells you, because nothing emails you when a booking
changes: **the school confirms times**, and if an appointment **has been changed
since it was booked, it says so**. If you looked last week, you can see what
moved.

> **[Screenshot: Appointments]**

## Messages

Conversations with the staff supporting your child. Text only.

## Resources

Materials a specialist has shared with your family, and where you confirm you
have received them.

## Academy

Courses, including *Empowered Parenting*, written for families.

## Library

Articles to read, as opposed to the Academy's courses to work through.

## Link a child

Where you add another child, or the first one, using a code from the school.

The code is issued to **a named person at a named address**, works **once**,
and expires after 30 days. There is deliberately **no search, no suggestions
and no "did you mean"** — every helpful touch there would be a way to confirm a
guess, and the thing being guessed at is a child's record.

A school's student ID is not accepted and never will be. It identifies a child;
it must never authorise anything.

> **[Screenshot: Link a child]**

## Privacy & Consent

Six consent items, per child. Each says **what it permits** and **what actually
changes if you withdraw it**, before it offers you the button.

**Two of the six change what the software does the moment you press:**

| Consent | What withdrawing it does |
|---|---|
| **AI-suggested classroom strategies** | Teachers stop receiving AI suggestions for your child **immediately**. They can still log behaviour, and everything else works as normal. |
| **Your child's own sign-in** | Their sign-in stops working **straight away**. The account is not deleted and their goals are untouched — they simply see nothing until you agree again. |

**The other four are records of an agreement your school keeps outside this
system** — storing your child's records, referral to a specialist, your own
access to the portal, and photos and media. Withdrawing one records your
request and the date; it does not act on its own. Removal of existing records,
or removing a specialist already assigned, is something you arrange with the
school directly. The screen labels these as records rather than dressing them
up as switches.

On the AI item, one detail worth knowing: when a teacher logs an incident, an
**anonymised** description may be sent to an AI service to suggest classroom
strategies. Your child's name, your family's names and any contact details are
removed before it is sent.

Withdrawing anything asks you to confirm first.

> **[Screenshot: Privacy & Consent]**

## Collab & Finance

Invoices from the school for your child, and the totals added up from them.

There is no annual budget ring, no expense breakdown by category and no
download button. An invoice carries one description and one amount; nothing in
the product categorises them or generates a PDF, so the screen adds up what is
really there rather than showing a chart of invented categories.

# Chapter 2 — Educators

You arrive by invitation from your school, and **two-factor authentication is
required** — set it up the first time you sign in. You see the students you are
assigned to, and no others.

## Dashboard

Your classroom overview. **Every number on it is a real count**; when a tile
reads zero, that is the truth about an empty table.

- **Needs you** — everything waiting on you, each item linking to where you
  would act. When there is nothing, it says *"Nothing is currently
  highlighted"*. If a check could not be run it says *"nothing waiting that
  could be checked"* instead, and names what it could not reach — because
  "nothing here" and "nothing was looked at" are different claims.
- **Logs in the last 24 hours**, at a glance.
- **Your students** — the roster, in reach from the landing screen.

> **[Screenshot: Educator Dashboard]**

## Students

Your roster, with **full names**. The abbreviated names used elsewhere protect
against a screen glanced at from across a room and against families seeing
other families' surnames; on your own roster they would just make it useless.

Search the list, or press **Add student**.

### Adding a student

Fill in first name, last name, year level, the school's own student ID, and
date of birth. If the student ID you type already exists, **you are warned
before you save** — two children with one reference is a problem that is far
easier to prevent than to unpick.

> **[Screenshot: Student roster]**

## Opening a student record

Click a row. The record has four parts:

- **Timeline** — one child's story in date order: behaviour history, what the
  family shared from home, specialist sessions and goal progress, merged into a
  single list instead of four you have to read in parallel.
- **Goals** — what the child is working towards, and progress against them.
- **Education plan** — whether they have an IEP, and **whether its review date
  has passed**, in the header rather than buried below. A goal on a child with
  an agreed plan is being worked towards something; a goal on a child without
  one is your own note. Only a live plan is ever called overdue — a closed plan
  past its review date *was* reviewed, and that is what closed it.
- **Guardian access** — who at home is linked to this child.

From here you also open the child's **IEP/ILP plans** (see below) and log
behaviour.

> **[Screenshot: a student record]**

## Logging behaviour

**The core task, designed to take under 20 seconds without taking your eyes off
the class.** Press **Log behaviour** on a student record.

1. **Select primary behaviour** — one of four:

   | | |
   |---|---|
   | **Disruptive** | Out of seat, shouting |
   | **Withdrawn** | Non-responsive, quiet |
   | **Emotional** | Crying, anxiety, frustration |
   | **Physical** | Pushing, throwing objects |

2. **Intensity level** — *Standard*, *Medium* or *High*.
3. **Save is enabled as soon as those two are chosen.** Everything below is
   optional.
4. **Observation notes** — optional on purpose. Requiring them under pressure
   guarantees they get skipped or filled with rubbish. Where your browser
   supports it, **Voice-to-text** dictates instead of typing; if it does not,
   the screen says so and typing works everywhere.

   Describe what you saw. **Avoid naming other students, and leave
   interpretation to the specialist.**

5. **Flag for safeguarding review** — a checkbox, kept separate from everything
   else and deliberately not automatic. It **sends this straight to a school
   administrator**. Use it whenever you think someone senior should see it —
   *you do not need to be certain.* Ticking it opens an optional *"What is your
   concern?"* box.

> **[Screenshot: the Quick Log dialog]**

### Logging with no connection

If you are offline the log is **saved on this device and not yet sent**, and
says so. A banner then appears **on every screen** until it goes through — not
just the one where you wrote it, because a log queued during period 3 on a tab
you have since closed would otherwise look exactly like a log that arrived.

## AI strategies

After a log is saved, MiZanova can suggest classroom strategies.

- **It only runs if the child's family has consented.** Without consent the
  request is refused before anything is sent.
- **No identifying details leave the system.** The child's name, family names
  and contact details are stripped first.
- **It gives classroom strategies, not clinical advice**, and offers a route to
  a human specialist.
- **Low-confidence suggestions never reach you.** They go to a specialist's
  review queue first, and until that specialist releases one it does not exist
  as far as your screen is concerned.

> **[Screenshot: the strategy panel with its privacy notice]**

## IEP and ILP plans

From a student record, open **the child's plans** — every IEP/ILP they have
had, newest first. The list leads with **whether a review date has passed**,
because that is the only row asking anything of you.

A plan is a **draft** while it is being written and can be edited freely. Once
it is **agreed** it becomes a record of what the school and family settled on,
and the screen renders it as a document rather than a form — the database
refuses changes to an agreed plan, so showing you an editable box would be a
lie told in advance.

**Agreeing to a plan cannot be undone from any screen.** It freezes the wording
and the goals and shows the plan to the family. You are asked to confirm first,
and the confirmation says what will actually happen.

The SMART criteria are printed beside the goal fields rather than checked by
the software. Nothing can tell whether a sentence is genuinely Specific or
Measurable; a rule that tried would reject good goals and pass bad ones, so the
criteria sit where the person writing can see them.

> **[Screenshot: the IEP editor, draft state]**

## Messages

Conversations with the families and colleagues of the children you teach.

The **student picker filters the whole screen**, not just the list of people
you can start a conversation with. *All students* is the default, because your
inbox spans your class; picking one narrows it.

## Schedule

**This is not a calendar, and deliberately so.** Nothing in MiZanova books
anything — there are no slots, no availability, no invitations and no
reminders, so a week grid would be a picture of a feature and every empty cell
would promise something could be dropped into it.

A teacher has exactly one kind of thing with a date on it: **goal target
dates**. That is what this screen is built from, and it says so. **Overdue
comes first and is not softened** — a goal past its target date is the one
thing here that needs acting on.

## Shared Resources, Academy and Library

- **Shared Resources** — materials a specialist has shared with children in
  your class.
- **Academy** — professional development courses for school staff.
- **Library** — articles and case studies to read.

# Chapter 3 — Specialists

You arrive by invitation, and **two-factor authentication is required**. You
see the students assigned to you as a specialist — the same request an educator
makes, answered differently by the database, which is why this screen cannot
widen it by accident.

## Command Centre

Ordered by what should interrupt your morning:

1. **Suggestions waiting on your judgement** — first, because until you act
   nothing reaches a teacher.
2. **Today's appointments.**
3. **Flagged incidents** on your caseload.
4. **Your caseload.**

Only *today* appears here. The Schedule screen owns booking, moving and writing
up, and is one click away.

> **[Screenshot: Specialist Command Centre]**

## Review Queue

**The human gate, and the most important screen you have.**

Every suggestion here was withheld from a teacher because the model was not
confident enough or asked for oversight. *Nothing here reaches a classroom
until you release it.*

For each one you can see the **anonymised input** that was actually sent —
stored so the anonymisation claim can be checked rather than just trusted.

- **Review note** (optional) — why you released or rejected it.
- **Release to teacher** — the suggestion becomes visible to them.
- **Reject** — it never appears anywhere again.

A queue like this only means something if it is actually worked. Until you act,
those suggestions do not exist as far as any teacher is concerned.

> **[Screenshot: Review Queue]**

## Caseload

The students assigned to you. Open one to reach the same record an educator
sees — timeline, goals, education plan — and the child's IEP/ILP plans, which
work exactly as described in [Chapter 2](#iep-and-ilp-plans).

## Schedule

Two halves, and they are different kinds of thing:

- **The calendar shows appointments** — these are *plans*.
- **The list underneath shows sessions** — these are *facts*.

**Completing an appointment is what writes a session**, so the delivered
figures at the top can only move when something is actually recorded. They
cannot be inflated by booking.

What is **not** built: availability, invitations and reminders. Your working
hours are recorded nowhere, so **an empty slot means nothing is booked in it,
not that anybody is free.** The note at the bottom of the screen says so.

> **[Screenshot: Specialist Schedule]**

## Resources

Upload a file and share it with a child's family and staff. **You see what you
uploaded**; a teacher, a family or a school administrator sees what has been
shared with a child they are entitled to.

Not built, and named on the screen rather than implied: importing from Drive,
automatic reminders, and anything described as "awaiting signature" — this
product records that a document was **read**, never that it was signed.

## Messages, Academy and Library

**Messages** — conversations about the children on your caseload, with their
families and teachers. **Academy** and **Library** — courses and articles.

# Chapter 4 — School administrators

Your account is set up by Special Miles. **Two-factor authentication is
required.** You see everyone and every child at your school.

## Command Centre

Two figures, both real and checkable: **how many flagged incidents are open**,
and **how long the oldest has been waiting**. The second is the one that
matters — a safeguarding queue is only as good as its slowest item.

There is no "audit readiness score". Nothing in the system could compute one,
and a confident compliance number with no basis is worse than none, because
somebody will quote it in a meeting.

> **[Screenshot: School Admin Command Centre]**

## Safeguarding

The queue of incidents teachers have flagged.

**It shows the child's name.** A safeguarding lead whose queue hides which
child is involved cannot follow anything up, ring a parent, or check on the
student. Anonymisation in this product is about what leaves for the AI and what
appears in aggregate reporting — not about hiding a child from the person
responsible for their welfare.

**There is no emergency button.** A red button implying the software can summon
help would be dangerous when all it does is write a database row. Emergencies
are handled by ringing someone, and the page says so.

> **[Screenshot: Safeguarding queue]**

## Students, and putting children on the roll

**Add students** gives you three ways in, because they are the same job at
different scales:

1. **Type one child.**
2. **Paste a column out of Excel.**
3. **Choose a spreadsheet** (`.xlsx`).

All three produce rows, **every row gets a verdict, and nothing is written
until you have seen the preview.** These are children's records — an import
that ran on submit and reported afterwards would leave no moment to notice a
shifted column or a date read the American way round.

> **[Screenshot: the import preview]**

## People, and Directory & Access

Two screens, deliberately separate:

- **People** answers questions — *who is this person, what are they to us, what
  are they connected to.* Everyone at the school including **parents**, who
  appear through the children that connect them, with each row saying which
  child that is. Searched and paged by the database, so a large school does not
  quietly show you a partial list.
- **Directory & Access** is for doing things — inviting staff, linking a
  guardian, assigning a caseload.

Neither shows a compliance score, a last-seen time or an activity count. **Not
one of those numbers exists in this system**, and inventing them is how a
directory becomes something people make decisions from. What is shown is role,
verification, vetting where it applies, and connections.

One control worth understanding: **removing a staff member's assignment
genuinely revokes their access to that child's records.** There is no switch
that claims to cut off platform access altogether — disabling an account needs
a key no browser may hold, and a toggle that looked like it worked while doing
nothing would be dangerous on exactly this screen.

> **[Screenshot: People]**

## Performance KPIs

Figures that trace to rows you could count yourself. The page says what is
missing and why.

MiZanova stores no attendance data, runs no satisfaction survey and holds no
quarterly baseline — so there is no absenteeism correlation, no parent
engagement score and no "escalation reduction vs last quarter". Every one of
those would be typed in by hand.

## Compliance

**Where you record consent a family gave you on paper.**

This matters more than it looks: the AI pipeline genuinely refuses to run
without consent, and before this screen existed only a parent could record one.
A school holding a signed paper form had no way to enter it, so students sat
with no consent at all and their teachers got a refusal they could do nothing
about.

Not here, and named on the screen: overdue documents, upcoming deadlines,
missing signatures, service minutes, therapy delivery percentages, report
drafting and reminders. None of that data exists, and two of them are things
this product deliberately does not do.

## Record Access

**Who opened which child's record**, in the order it happened.

This exists to answer one question, asked by a parent or a regulator after
something has gone wrong: *"which staff members have looked at my child's
file?"* It is deliberately plain — no summarising, no scoring, no "unusual
activity" flag, because a number claiming to detect suspicion would be
inventing one, and this is the screen where that would matter most.

It is the one record that **cannot be filled in retroactively**: it exists only
from the day it started.

## Messages

**This inbox works in one direction, and the screen says so.**

You can open a conversation with any family or teacher at your school, and
replies come back here. **Nobody can open one with you.** That is not an
oversight — the rule requiring the other person to be a guardian or educator of
a specific child is what stops staff at one school messaging a family at
another, and loosening it to make this inbox symmetrical would change a privacy
boundary to suit a screen.

## Invoices

**Two steps, on purpose.** An invoice is created as a **draft** and **issued**
separately. A family cannot see a draft, so a wrong amount is a private mistake
rather than a bill somebody has already read.

Amounts are typed in dollars.

- **Edit** and **Discard** appear on drafts and on nothing else.
- An **issued** invoice is cancelled, never deleted.
- A **paid** invoice cannot be touched.

> **[Screenshot: Invoices, showing a draft and an issued invoice]**

## Academy and Library

Courses and articles, as for every other role.

# Chapter 5 — Platform administrators

Special Miles staff. **Two-factor authentication is required.** You can see
across every school.

## Global Overview

**A work queue, not a scoreboard.** Everything on it is something a person at
Special Miles can act on today, and every item links to where they would act.

The other two questions have their own screens: *how is each tenant doing* is
**Schools**, and *what money has moved* is **Billing**.

> **[Screenshot: Global Overview]**

## Schools

Each tenant and how it is going. Ages are shown in days rather than raw hours,
because the point of the column is that somebody sees a problem without doing
arithmetic first.

Clicking a school opens **who is in it**.

## Enquiries

People who asked to become customers from the pricing page. **The only screen
in the product showing people with no account** — there is no profile to look
them up in, so what they typed is shown in full rather than summarised.

It is a platform admin screen rather than a school one because these rows hold
names and contact details of people at organisations that are not customers
yet, quite possibly including a competitor school.

## Billing & Revenue, and Subscriptions

**Two kinds of money, and two separate screens.**

- **Billing & Revenue** — a *school* invoicing a *family* for a named child.
- **Subscriptions** — *Special Miles* invoicing a *school*.

Different payers; a combined total would mean nothing. There is no ARR, MRR or
churn, because nobody subscribes to MiZanova in that sense.

**This screen cannot mark anything paid.** Payment status is reachable only
through the payment provider, never from a browser session.

## Specialist Applications

Reviewing applications to join the network — checking two numbers against two
public registers, and recording that somebody did. The register links are on
each card, because a reviewer who has to go and find them will eventually stop.

Two things stated on the screen itself:

- **The button is the attestation, not the check.** MiZanova does not talk to
  the Office of the Children's Guardian or to AHPRA. Approving records that a
  named member of Special Miles staff completed those checks, on a date.
- **Approval creates no account.** "Approved" reads like "they're in", and the
  truth is one step short of that — the account waits for a school.

> **[Screenshot: Specialist Applications]**

## Screening

**The screen that goes looking, so nobody has to remember to.** Approval is a
statement about the past; checks expire and checks are revoked, and this makes
the present visible.

- **Missing checks come first** — a list of expiring checks answers *"whose is
  running out?"* and silently omits *"whose do we not hold at all?"*, which is
  the more urgent question because there is nothing to expire.
- **Ordered worst-first and deliberately not filterable.** A filter is a way to
  look at a subset and feel finished.
- **It revokes nothing.** An expired check does not end a membership or remove
  an assignment by itself — a person decides that.

## Staff Verification

Educators, specialists **and** school administrators all appear here, and all
three need verifying before they can see a child's record.

As with applications, **the button is the attestation, not the check**. This
does not read an uploaded ID or query a Working With Children Check; those are
real-world processes done outside the software. Nobody can verify themselves —
the field cannot be written from a browser at all.

## Courses and Articles

The Academy and Library content management.

- **Everything starts as a draft** and is invisible to its audience until
  published. Withdrawing afterwards is possible, but a family who already read
  it has read it.
- **The audience is a role**, and choosing none is refused.
- **Publishing a case study requires an explicit confirmation that the people
  in it agreed.** Special Miles' own material describes work with named schools
  and families, and a CMS that let somebody publish *"how we supported a Year 3
  student with selective mutism at Parramatta West"* is a CMS that can identify
  a child. The database enforces this, not just the form.

## AI Governance

What a platform administrator can genuinely exercise:

- **Stop the AI entirely.**
- **Change how cautious the routing is** — how much confidence is needed before
  a suggestion reaches a teacher rather than a specialist's review queue.
- **Read who changed what, and why.** Every change demands a written reason,
  enforced by the database rather than by the form.

There is no accuracy percentage, no "deploy new model", no model version
history and no bias monitor. MiZanova calls a hosted model rather than training
one, and deliberately collects no demographic data — so a bias monitor would
have nothing to monitor and an accuracy figure would be invented.

> **[Screenshot: AI Governance]**

## Audit Log and Record Access

**Audit Log** — who did what, when and why, as a table with columns, because an
audit trail is consulted with a question already in mind and scanned rather
than read.

**Record Access** — the oversight layer above each school's own access log.
Staff are visible to their school, and the school is visible to here. A school
administrator cannot see their own accesses, which is what makes the chain
real.

# Chapter 6 — Students

## Why there is one screen

Your account shows you **the goals you are working on at school**, and that is
the whole of it.

This is a deliberate decision, not an unfinished one. A student account is not
a small version of a parent's. You do not see behaviour notes written about
you, anything from the safeguarding record, your plan documents, messages
between adults, or anything about any other student. A menu item leading to an
empty page would suggest the rest was coming, so there isn't one.

## My goals

What you are working towards, and how it is going.

## Academy and Library

Courses and articles written for you — including executive functioning, study
skills and self-advocacy.

## How you get in, and how it can stop

Your parent or guardian agrees to your sign-in, and **they can withdraw that at
any time**. If they do, your sign-in stops working straight away. Your account
is not deleted and your goals are untouched — you simply see nothing until they
agree again.
